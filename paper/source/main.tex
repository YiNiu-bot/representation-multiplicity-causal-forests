\title{Representation Multiplicity in Causal Forests}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent Feature-subsampled causal forests draw random sets of covariate columns, even
when several columns encode the same split action. Equivalent recodings can
therefore alter candidate-split
probabilities without changing covariate information. I characterize the
forest target by the cumulative resolution intensity of an unresolved effect
component along a tree path. In a randomized two-modifier design, growing
blocks of strictly increasing recodings make the scalar
\texttt{causal\_forest} path in \texttt{grf} 2.4.0 converge to
representation-dependent targets whose signs disagree for half the population.
Every fixed finite representation covered by the theorem recovers the CATE
under the stated conditions. Simulations and a
cash-transfer ranking audit show that recoding sensitivity can greatly exceed
seed sensitivity. A class-sampled forest restores exact invariance on a
declared finite array under canonical routing and common class-level randomness.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} causal forests; feature subsampling; policy learning;
randomized search; representation invariance; redundant covariates.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{JEL Classification:} C14, C21, C55
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Suppose two analysts fit the same causal forest to the same data. The first
includes household size. The second retains household size together with its
logarithm and several split-equivalent recodings. The two representations
contain the same information and induce the same threshold partitions, but
feature subsampling treats their columns as separate candidates. Household size
therefore receives several chances to enter the split search. Additional trees
only estimate the resulting representation-specific infinite-forest limit more
precisely.

This distinction matters when forests are used to rank treatment effects. A
modifier affects a leaf prediction after the tree selects a split that
separates its relevant states. Candidate inclusion alone does not suffice,
because a stronger unresolved modifier may win the split. The probability of
selection accumulates over the nodes reached before the leaf stops. I call the
sum of these history-conditional selection probabilities the component's
cumulative resolution intensity. Its exact resolution probability
is one minus the expected survival product. When every one-node probability vanishes, a
limiting intensity \(\lambda\) gives resolution probability
\(1-e^{-\lambda}\).

The main result, Theorem~\ref{thm:adaptive}, studies the scalar
\texttt{causal\_forest} path in \texttt{grf} 2.4.0 under a two-modifier
randomized design. One representation repeats the weaker modifier and leaves
the stronger modifier as a singleton. The other reverses their roles. Both
representations generate the same sigma-field at every sample size. When the
number of recodings grows fast enough relative to path depth, the singleton is
unlikely to be selected before stopping. The two forests then converge to
different component targets, and their signs disagree for half the population.
With fixed \texttt{mtry}, the boundary is proportional to path depth. Under the
package-default \texttt{mtry} scaling, within the same asymptotic learner
sequence, it is proportional to squared path depth. Every fixed finite
representation eventually lies on the recovery side of the boundary. The
inconsistent limits therefore concern growing representation sequences. The
same mechanism applies to any fixed number of signed effect modifiers.

The proof uses the general target formula in Theorem~\ref{thm:opportunity}.
For an honest score forest, a component with limiting cumulative intensity
\(\lambda\) enters the target with coefficient \(1-e^{-\lambda}\). This
coefficient measures the attenuation caused by incomplete access to the
component. Applying the formula requires
learner-specific leaf, aggregation, and hazard arguments. Section~\ref{sec:source}
provides those arguments for the signed \texttt{grf} design.

The theorem permits exact copies and deterministic strictly increasing
recodings, which preserve the source split library and routing rule. The
simulations and Kenya exercise ask how large the resulting sensitivity can be
in finite samples. In the
highest-dimensional Monte Carlo design, split-equivalent recodings produce
opposite-signed CATE estimates for 46.2 percent of evaluation observations,
compared with 0.09 percent after changing only the forest seed. In public data
from the Kenya cash-transfer experiment of Egger et al.\ \citeyearpar{egger2022},
author-constructed preprocessing-style bundles change selected-set membership
for 5.9 to 7.5 percent of households under a hypothetical top-half ranking.
Both exercises compare the stability of fitted rankings across representations.
Assessing accuracy or welfare would require a different design. The recodings
were constructed for this paper and did not appear in the original study.

The paper also develops a class-sampled forest for a declared finite array. It
certifies columns that generate the same threshold actions on a declared array
and samples the resulting classes instead of raw columns. Under class-indexed
randomness and canonical routing, Proposition~\ref{prop:quotient} gives exact
equality of partitions, predictions, reported standard errors, and selected
sets on that array. Extending this equality to new covariate values requires an
additional routing rule.

Earlier work documents the underlying candidate-set effect. Duplicate columns
enter randomized candidate sets more often and can change prediction or
variable importance
\citep{louppe2014,strobl2007,tolosi2011,gregorutti2017,kubus2018}.
Existing remedies reduce the influence of redundant features.
\citet{chen2013module} sample modules before choosing representative variables,
whereas \citet{colot2021} weight features inversely by estimated cluster size.
These studies establish the duplicate-column diagnosis and motivate group-aware
feature sampling. The question here is how the same mechanism changes
causal-forest targets.

High-dimensional forest theory allows the ambient dimension, candidate budget,
and path depth to vary. Its recovery bounds depend on candidate access along a path
\citep{chi2022high,klusowski2024large,mei2026rosa}. The index
\(\mathcal I_n=H_n\E Q_{M_n}/M_n\) is the corresponding feature-access measure
for the source law studied here, where \(H_n\) is path depth, \(Q_{M_n}\) is the
nodewise candidate count, and \(M_n\) is the number of raw columns. The paired
representations in Theorem~\ref{thm:adaptive} generate the
same sigma-field but assign different access probabilities to the same split
actions, and the theorem traces this difference into representation-specific
causal-forest targets, sign reversals, regret, and a sharp multiplicity boundary
for \texttt{grf}. Theorem~\ref{thm:opportunity} maps resolution hazards into
target coefficients, and Theorem~\ref{thm:adaptive} verifies its conditions for
the source learner.

Proposition~\ref{prop:quotient} sharpens the established grouping remedy. It
uses exact split-action certificates and a coupling that preserves every listed
forest output on a declared finite array. The result differs from approximate
correlation clusters and from exhaustive-CART invariance to monotone recoding
\citep{hong2026}. Related work on forest randomization, honest inference,
pointwise convergence, and accuracy provides further background
\citep{biau2012,klusowski2021,mei2026,wager2018,athey2019,
cattaneo2022pointwise,cattaneo2025accuracy}. The treatment-ranking
interpretation follows \citet{hirano2009}, \citet{kitagawa2018}, and
\citet{athey2021}.

Section~\ref{sec:framework} develops the hazard formula. Section~\ref{sec:source}
maps it to the signed \texttt{grf} design and derives the multiplicity boundary.
Section~\ref{sec:quotient} presents the class-sampled correction, and
Section~\ref{sec:evidence} reports the simulation and cash-transfer diagnostics.
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} contains the proofs and source verification. The
replication archive documents every simulation and empirical recoding.

\section{Resolution Hazards and Forest Targets}\label{sec:framework}

Let \(\{(Y_i,W_i,X_i)\}_{i=1}^n\) be an i.i.d. training sample, where \(Y_i\)
is the observed outcome, \(W_i\in\{0,1\}\) is treatment, and \(X_i\) contains
the covariates, and let \(\mathcal D_n\) denote this sample. Let \(Y_i(w)\)
denote the potential outcome under treatment
\(w\), and let \(P_X\) denote the marginal law of \(X_i\). Define
\[
\tau(x)=\E\{Y_i(1)-Y_i(0)\mid X_i=x\}.
\]

At each internal node, a tree draws candidate covariate columns, evaluates
their admissible threshold splits, and selects one using the stated criterion
and tie rule. A forest averages predictions across randomized trees. We call
this recursive procedure \emph{tree search}.

Let \(\mathcal X\) denote the support of \(X\). A raw representation is a
measurable map from \(\mathcal X\) to \(\mathbb R^M\). We write it as
\(R=(R_1,\ldots,R_M)\), with \(R(x)=(R_1(x),\ldots,R_M(x))\). Throughout the
asymptotic results,
\(R=R_n\) and \(M=M_n\) may vary with \(n\). The index is suppressed when it
does not aid the exposition. Two representations are information equivalent if
\begin{equation*}
\sigma\{R(X)\}=\sigma\{\widetilde R(X)\}.
\end{equation*}
Tree search requires a more restrictive equivalence. At a node containing
index set \(I\), column \(j\) generates the unordered threshold-split library
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal H_j(I)=
\left\{
\{L,I\setminus L\}:L=\{i\in I:R_j(X_i)\le a\},\ a\in\R
\right\},
\end{equation*}
under declared tie and missing-value rules. Columns with the same library at
every reachable node are \emph{split equivalent}. The resulting equivalence
classes are the split-action classes, and \(G\) denotes their number.

Three notions of equivalence will be useful. Information equivalence compares
sigma-fields. Split equivalence compares the actions available to tree search.
Finite-array certification, introduced in Section~\ref{sec:quotient}, verifies
split equivalence only on a declared array. Exact copies satisfy all three
notions on every array, but the converses need not hold.

The signed design in Section~\ref{sec:source} provides the concrete mechanism.
One of two effect components receives \(c_n\) raw labels and the other remains a
singleton. If the singleton enters the candidate set at a node with probability
\(\omega_n\) and a target path has order \(H_n\) internal nodes, then
\(\mathcal I_n=H_n\omega_n\) is its cumulative-inclusion index. Vanishing
\(\mathcal I_n\) leads to omission, while diverging \(\mathcal I_n\) leads to
recovery. The abstract result below expresses the same idea through
history-conditional successful-resolution hazards.

To state the abstract transfer result, consider a scalar honest-score forest.
Each tree uses a splitting sample to construct an adaptive partition from random
subsets of representation columns and an independent estimation sample to
compute its leaf moment. Let \(\Theta\) denote the randomization of a generic
tree. The observation, covariate, tree-state, and randomization spaces are
standard Borel. All split, tie, stopping, leaf, score, and prediction maps are
jointly measurable in their displayed arguments.
Under representation \(R\), let \(I_n^{\rm est}\) be its estimation
indices and let \(L_n^R(x)\) be the terminal cell containing \(x\).
Let \(\mathcal Z_n\) be the sigma-field generated by a standard-Borel random
element containing the score-training folds, fitted nuisance functions, and
their training randomization. Let \(\Gamma_n(y,w,x)\) be a scalar score map
whose randomness is \(\mathcal Z_n\)-measurable, and
write \(\Gamma_{i,n}=\Gamma_n(Y_i,W_i,X_i)\), with the fold for observation
\(i\) understood when scores are cross-fitted. Let \((Y',W',X')\) be a fresh
observation, independent of the training sample and all nuisance and tree
randomization. Define
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
N_n^{R,{\rm est}}(x)
&=\sum_{i\in I_n^{\rm est}}
\one\{X_i\in L_n^R(x)\},\\
\widetilde T_n^R(x)
&=\frac{1}{N_n^{R,{\rm est}}(x)}
\sum_{i\in I_n^{\rm est}}
\one\{X_i\in L_n^R(x)\}\Gamma_{i,n}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Set \(\widetilde T_n^R(x)=0\) on an empty estimation leaf. Here
\(N_n^{R,{\rm est}}(x)\) is the number of estimation observations in the
terminal cell containing \(x\), and \(\widetilde T_n^R(x)\) is their average
score. Empty estimation
leaves are pruned, so the denominator is positive whenever a tree contributes
at \(x\). Conditional means on population-null leaves may be assigned any
fixed version because the statements below are integrated with respect to
\(P_X\). For \(b=1,\ldots,B_n\), append the index \(b\) to these objects when
they are generated by tree \(b\). Let
\(\widehat T_n^R:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R\) be the prediction returned by the
\(B_n\)-tree forest.

\begin{assumption}\label{ass:semantic-score}
Along the representation sequence \(R_n\), there exist a fixed finite number
\(G\), a constant \(\mu\), and measurable functions
\(f_1,\ldots,f_G:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R\) satisfying
\(\E f_g(X)=0\) for \(g=1,\ldots,G\) and
\(\sum_{g=1}^G\|f_g\|_\infty<\infty\), and a function
\(T^*:\mathcal X\to\mathbb R\) such that
\begin{equation*}
\begin{gathered}
T^*(x)=\mu+\sum_{g=1}^G f_g(x),\\
\left\|\E\{\Gamma_n(Y',W',X')\mid X'=\cdot,\mathcal Z_n\}-T^*\right\|_\infty=o_p(1).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation*}
\end{assumption}

Here \(T^*\) is the limiting conditional score mean. Centering the components
sets \(\mu=\E T^*(X)\), and \(f_g\) records the deviation associated with
split-action class \(g\). The decomposition is tied to the declared classes and
cannot be recovered from \(T^*\) without that structure. The uniform approximation requires the conditional score
mean to approach \(T^*\) over \(\mathcal X\), while the bound on
\(\sum_g\|f_g\|_\infty\) keeps the component sum uniformly bounded. In the
signed design below, \(T^*=\tau_a\) and the two components are
\(a_1s_1\) and \(a_2s_2\). More generally, a component
may be an effect modifier, a regression component, a conditional-moment
component, or another feature of a scalar generalized-forest score.

For a target \(x\) and split-action class \(g\), a split \emph{resolves} the class
when it uses that class's threshold action, making the target's descendant leaf
locally constant in the corresponding component. Candidate availability alone
is not resolution. A failure history records the entire unresolved path up to
arrival at the current node. At any nonterminal history,
\(\rho_{g,n,t}^{R,0}(x)\) is the conditional probability that the next tree
transition resolves \(g\). Let \(K_{g,n,t}^{R,0}\) be the regular conditional
law of the next history given failure to resolve \(g\). Complete-history
conditioning imposes no Markov restriction. Standard Borelness gives jointly
measurable versions of the finite collection of kernels. If the current hazard
is one, that node remains in the product with zero failure factor and the next
state is absorbing. Histories reachable only after an earlier null failure are
assigned zero hazard and the absorbing transition.
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} records the construction.

The potential no-resolution path starts from the original root law and evolves
according to \(K_{g,n,t}^{R,0}\). On every positive-probability branch it retains
the raw dimension, candidate law, stopping rule, and competing actions of the
original tree. Let \(\mathcal F_{g,n,t-1}^{R,0}(x)\) contain the complete
failure history upon arrival at node \(t\). The event that node \(t\) is reached
and its current state are \(\mathcal F_{g,n,t-1}^{R,0}(x)\)-measurable, making
the hazard predictable. Each split sends the target to a strict subset of the
finite splitting sample, and a null failure branch is absorbing. The path
therefore terminates after finitely many internal nodes. Denote this number by
\(J_{g,n}^{R,0}(x)\).

On the actual target path, let \(S_{g,n,t}^R(x)\) indicate that the split at
internal node \(t\) resolves class \(g\), and set it to zero after the path
stops. The success rule is part of the forest specification. Define
\begin{equation*}
D_{g,n}^R(x)=\one\{S_{g,n,t}^R(x)=1\text{ for some }t\ge1\}.
\end{equation*}
A class can enter the candidate set without being selected for the split.
Sequential conditioning gives the exact failure-path identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:survival-product}
\E_\Theta\{1-D_{g,n}^R(x)\mid\mathcal D_n,\mathcal Z_n\}
=\E_{\Theta^0}\!\left[
\prod_{t=1}^{J_{g,n}^{R,0}(x)}
\{1-\rho_{g,n,t}^{R,0}(x)\}
\middle|\mathcal D_n,\mathcal Z_n\right],
\end{equation}
for \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\), where \(\Theta^0\) denotes potential
failure-path randomization. The product equals one when
\(J_{g,n}^{R,0}(x)=0\).
Define the cumulative intensity and maximal one-node hazard by
\begin{equation*}
\Lambda_{g,n}^R(x)
=\sum_{t=1}^{J_{g,n}^{R,0}(x)}\rho_{g,n,t}^{R,0}(x),
\qquad
\bar\rho_{g,n}^R(x)
=\max_{t\le J_{g,n}^{R,0}(x)}\rho_{g,n,t}^{R,0}(x),
\end{equation*}
with both quantities zero when \(J_{g,n}^{R,0}(x)=0\). Thus
\(\Lambda_{g,n}^R\) is a sum of conditional success probabilities, while the
exact conditional failure probability is the expected product in
\eqref{eq:survival-product}.

Expectations bearing the subscript \(\Theta\) or \(\Theta^0\) integrate over
actual-tree or potential failure-path randomization,
and \(\Prb_X\) denotes probability for an independent evaluation covariate with
law \(P_X\). All integrals and \(L^p(P_X)\) norms below use the same evaluation
law. For a measurable limiting function
\(\lambda_g^R:\mathcal X\to[0,\infty]\), use the convention
\(e^{-\infty}=0\). Convergence to an extended-real limit is understood after
the transformation \(u\mapsto u/(1+u)\).

\begin{assumption}\label{ass:honest-resolution}
Along the sequence \(R=R_n\), the following conditions hold uniformly over the
fixed finite set of split-action classes.
\begin{enumerate}
\renewcommand{\labelenumi}{(\roman{enumi})}
\item \emph{Honest leaf and component reduction.} The honest leaf statistic estimates its score
mean.
\begin{multline}\label{eq:honest-score-leaf}
\E_\Theta\Bigg[
\int\left|\widetilde T_n^R(x)-
\E\{\Gamma_n(Y',W',X')\mid X'\in L_n^R(x),
\mathcal D_n,\Theta,\mathcal Z_n\}
\right|dP_X(x)\\
\Bigm|\mathcal D_n,\mathcal Z_n\Bigg]=o_p(1).
\end{multline}
For each component, the conditional leaf mean approaches \(f_g(x)\) after
resolution and zero before resolution, in the following integrated sense.
\begin{multline}\label{eq:component-leaf-reduction}
\sum_{g=1}^G\E_\Theta\Bigg[
\int\left|
\E\{f_g(X')\mid X'\in L_n^R(x),\mathcal D_n,\Theta,\mathcal Z_n\}
-D_{g,n}^R(x)f_g(x)
\right|dP_X(x)\\
\Bigm|\mathcal D_n,\mathcal Z_n\Bigg]=o_p(1).
\end{multline}
\item \emph{Forest aggregation.} The reported forest admits the honest
linearization
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-forest-linearization}
\left\|\widehat T_n^R-
\frac1{B_n}\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}\widetilde T_{b,n}^R
\right\|_{L^1(P_X)}=o_p(1),
\qquad B_n\to\infty,
\end{equation}
and, conditional on \((\mathcal D_n,\mathcal Z_n)\), every tree has the same
marginal law as the generic tree \(\Theta\). Under this same conditioning, the
tree randomizers are independent or form mutually independent groups of
uniformly bounded size. For some
constant \(C<\infty\),
\[
\sup_{n,b}\E_\Theta\!\left[
\|\widetilde T_{b,n}^R\|_{L^2(P_X)}^2
\middle|\mathcal D_n,\mathcal Z_n\right]\le C
\quad\text{with probability tending to one}.
\]
\item \emph{Hazard convergence.} Under the joint law of
\((\mathcal D_n,\mathcal Z_n)\), an independent \(X\sim P_X\), and a fresh
potential failure path generated by the preceding kernels,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hazard-limit-condition}
\Lambda_{g,n}^R(X)\longrightarrow_p\lambda_g^R(X),
\qquad
\bar\rho_{g,n}^R(X)\longrightarrow_p0
\quad\text{on }\{\lambda_g^R(X)<\infty\},
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{ass:honest-resolution} gives the estimator conditions used in
Theorem~\ref{thm:opportunity}. Part (i) links honest leaves to the conditional
score mean and records whether each component has been resolved. Part (ii)
approximates the reported forest by the average of its linearized trees. Part
(iii) requires convergence of the cumulative resolution hazard and excludes a
nonnegligible one-node hazard when that limit is finite. Section~\ref{sec:source}
verifies these conditions for the scalar \texttt{grf} path and design stated
there.

Assumptions~\ref{ass:semantic-score} and \ref{ass:honest-resolution}(i) imply
the component leaf reduction
\begin{equation}\label{eq:semantic-leaf-reduction}
\E_\Theta\left[
\left\|\widetilde T_n^R-
\mu-\sum_{g=1}^GD_{g,n}^R f_g\right\|_{L^1(P_X)}
\middle|\mathcal D_n,\mathcal Z_n\right]=o_p(1).
\end{equation}

The next theorem isolates the probabilistic step that is common across
learners. Section~\ref{sec:source} verifies its assumptions for the scalar
\texttt{grf} learner and obtains the source-specific result in
Theorem~\ref{thm:adaptive}.

\begin{theorem}[Resolution hazards and forest targets]\label{thm:opportunity}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:semantic-score} and
\ref{ass:honest-resolution},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-opportunity-target}
\left\|\widehat T_n^R-
\left[\mu+\sum_{g=1}^G
\{1-e^{-\lambda_g^R(\cdot)}\}f_g\right]
\right\|_{L^1(P_X)}\longrightarrow_p0.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\pagebreak[3]
The coefficient function on \(f_g\) is
\(1-e^{-\lambda_g^R(\cdot)}\). It vanishes with the hazard, lies strictly
between zero and one for a finite positive hazard, and approaches one as the
hazard diverges.
Information-equivalent representations share a target when their componentwise
hazards share a limit. Different hazard limits can produce different targets.

\section{A Signed \texttt{grf} Design}\label{sec:source}

\subsection{Design and representations}

For the signed forest result, let \(X=(X_1,X_2)\sim
\operatorname{Unif}([0,1]^2)\), set
\[
\iota_j(X)=\one\{X_j\ge1/2\},
\qquad s_j(X)=2\iota_j(X)-1,
\]
and fix constants \(0<\underline a<\overline a<\infty\) and
\(0<\Delta_a<\overline a-\underline a\). The coefficient region is
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal C_a=
\left\{(a_1,a_2):
\underline a\le a_1<a_2\le\overline a,
\ a_2-a_1\ge\Delta_a
\right\},
\end{equation*}
and
\begin{equation*}
\tau_a(X)=a_1s_1(X)+a_2s_2(X).
\end{equation*}
For each \(n\) and \(a\in\mathcal C_a\), let
\(\{(Y_{i,n},W_{i,n},X_{i,n})\}_{i=1}^n\) be independent and identically
distributed under \(P_a\) according to the randomized model
\begin{equation}\label{eq:production-dgp}
Y(w)=m_0(X)+w\tau_a(X)+U,\qquad \E(U\mid X,W)=0,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation*}
W\perp\{Y(0),Y(1)\}\mid X,\qquad
\Prb(W=1\mid X)=\frac12,\qquad Y=Y(W).
\end{equation*}
Here \(m_0\) is a measurable baseline outcome function and \(U\) is the
disturbance.
The function \(m_0\) and the conditional law of \(U\) given \((X,W)\) are held
fixed as \(a\) varies over \(\mathcal C_a\).
We suppress the triangular-array subscript on observations and write \(P_X\)
for the \(X\)-marginal of \(P_a\). Cross-fitting fold labels, tree subsample
indices, honesty allocations, and node-level candidate draws are generated
independently of the observations. A fixed representation-independent order
resolves any exact criterion ties. A
cross-fitted nuisance prediction for observation \(i\) excludes the fold
containing \(i\). These randomizations are mutually independent except when a
common-random-number coupling is explicitly used to compare two marginally
identical learner specifications.
The assignment probability \(e(X)=1/2\) is known. Write
\(m_a(X)=\E(Y\mid X)=m_0(X)+\tau_a(X)/2\) and
\begin{equation*}
\psi_\theta(Y,W,X;m_a)
=(W-1/2)\{Y-m_a(X)-\theta(W-1/2)\}.
\end{equation*}
For a parent cell \(C\), the population solution is
\(\theta_C=\E\{\tau_a(X)\mid X\in C\}\), its Jacobian is
\(A_C=\E\{(W-e(X))^2\mid X\in C\}=1/4\), and the GRF linearized score
\(\phi_C(Y,W,X)=A_C^{-1}\psi_{\theta_C}(Y,W,X;m_a)\) satisfies
\begin{equation*}
\E\{\phi_C(Y,W,X)\mid X=x\}=\tau_a(x)-\theta_C.
\end{equation*}

For \(g\in\{1,2\}\), let \(c_n\ge1\) be an integer. For
\(\ell=1,\ldots,c_n\), let \(\varphi_{g\ell n}:[0,1]\to\mathbb R\) be a
deterministic strictly increasing map with finite values, and set
\(X_g^{(\ell)}=\varphi_{g\ell n}(X_g)\). The identity map gives an exact copy.
Define
\begin{align*}
R_{1,n}(X)
&=\{X_1^{(1)},\ldots,X_1^{(c_n)},X_2\},\\
R_{2,n}(X)
&=\{X_1,X_2^{(1)},\ldots,X_2^{(c_n)}\}.
\end{align*}
Thus \(M_n=c_n+1\). Strict monotonicity gives the same threshold partitions for
every column in a repeated block. In exact arithmetic it also preserves source
routing because
\(\varphi_{g\ell n}(x)\le\varphi_{g\ell n}(t)\) if and only if \(x\le t\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:quotient} treats more general recodings on a declared
array under canonical class routing.
For every \(n\),
\begin{equation*}
\sigma\{R_{1,n}(X)\}=\sigma\{R_{2,n}(X)\}=\sigma(X_1,X_2).
\end{equation*}
Representation \(R_{1,n}\) repeats the weaker modifier \(X_1\) and leaves the
stronger modifier \(X_2\) as a singleton. Representation \(R_{2,n}\) repeats
the stronger modifier and leaves the weaker one as a singleton. The main
comparison asks whether a singleton is selected before the target path reaches
its leaf.
In this signed design, each effect component corresponds one-to-one to a
split-action class. We use \emph{component} for the term in the CATE and
\emph{class} for the collection of column labels sampled by the tree.

\subsection{Source learner and verification objects}

Each tree draws candidate columns, evaluates their admissible threshold actions,
chooses the largest source criterion, and repeats until the stopping rule is
met. An independent honest sample then computes the leaf moment. The learner
follows the causal-forest source code in \texttt{grf} version 2.4.0 at commit
\texttt{11ead50b}, with unit sample weights,
\texttt{W.hat=1/2}, \texttt{stabilize.splits=TRUE}, and zero imbalance
penalty \citep{grfsource240}. Each tree draws \(s_n\) observations without
replacement and divides them honestly into splitting and estimation samples
whose fractions are bounded away from zero. At a node with \(M\) representation
columns, the source candidate-count rule is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:grf-q}
N_M\sim\Pois(\xi_M),\qquad Q_M=\max\{1,\min(N_M,M)\},
\end{equation}
where \(\xi_M=\texttt{mtry}\) \citep{grfreference}. Conditional on \(Q_M=q\),
the implementation samples \(q\) columns uniformly without replacement.
At each node it evaluates the admissible thresholds of those columns and uses a
fixed tie order. For probability calculations, the independent node draws can
be indexed in advance by tree and node address. This indexing is an analytical
device and leaves the source learner's distribution unchanged. The package
continues to use its sequential pseudorandom number generator.
Let \(k_n\) be the source minimum node size, the required count on each side
of the parent treatment mean within each child. Write \(m=m_a\), and let
\(\widehat m\) be its cross-fitted estimator. We require
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nuisance-rate}
\|\widehat m-m\|_\infty=o_p(1),
\qquad \|\widehat m\|_\infty=O_p(1)
\end{equation}
uniformly over \(a\in\mathcal C_a\). Write
\[
\widetilde Y_i=Y_i-\widehat m(X_i),
\qquad Z_i=W_i-1/2\in\{-1/2,1/2\}.
\]
For a splitting node \(C\), write \(n_C=|C|\), let bars denote unweighted
node averages, and set
\begin{equation*}
\widehat A_C=\frac14-\bar Z_C^2,
\qquad
\widehat\theta_C
=\frac{\overline{Z\widetilde Y}_C-\bar Z_C\bar{\widetilde Y}_C}
{\widehat A_C}.
\end{equation*}
For a candidate split \((j,t)\), write
\(C_L(j,t)\) and \(C_R(j,t)\) for its children, and let
\(\mathcal Q_C(j,t)\) denote the corresponding population CART criterion.
This criterion is the child-size-weighted squared contrast in mean score
between the two children. We use \emph{split gain} for the value of this
criterion. Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} records the source binding
and verifies the exact scalar relabeling formula.
Suppress the arguments when clear. For \(A\in\{C_L,C_R\}\), write
\(n_A=|A|\) and
\(\widehat p_{A\mid C}=n_A/n_C\). Under the preceding specialization, the scalar
source relabeling and a rescaling of its split criterion are exactly
\begin{align}
\Psi_{i,C}
&=(Z_i-\bar Z_C)
\{\widetilde Y_i-\bar{\widetilde Y}_C
-\widehat\theta_C(Z_i-\bar Z_C)\},
\label{eq:source-relabel}\\
\widehat{\mathcal Q}_C^{\rm grf}(j,t)
&=16\widehat p_{C_L\mid C}\widehat p_{C_R\mid C}
\bigl(\bar\Psi_{C_L}-\bar\Psi_{C_R}\bigr)^2.\notag
\end{align}
The factor 16 does not affect the source argmax. The source option
\texttt{alpha} is the minimum information fraction. Write its fixed value as
\(\beta\in(0,1/4)\). At
node \(C\), let \(\mathcal S_n(C)\) contain each pair \((j,t)\) such that
\(j\) is sampled under \eqref{eq:grf-q}, \(t\) is one of the finite source
thresholds for column \(j\) in \(C\), and both children
\(A\in\{C_L(j,t),C_R(j,t)\}\) contain at least \(k_n\) observations with
\(Z_i<\bar Z_C\) and at least \(k_n\) with \(Z_i>\bar Z_C\), while satisfying
\(n_A(1/4-\bar Z_A^2)\ge\beta n_C(1/4-\bar Z_C^2)\).
The following condition applies to every node reachable under either
representation or along either potential failure path in the proof, for every
realized nonempty candidate set. We impose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-split-nondegeneracy}
\Prb\!\left\{
\max_{(j,t)\in\mathcal S_n(C)}\widehat{\mathcal Q}_C^{\rm grf}(j,t)>0
\text{ at every such }C
\text{ with }\mathcal S_n(C)\ne\emptyset
\right\}=1.
\end{equation}
The probability integrates over the sample, tree subsampling, honesty split,
nuisance estimates, outcomes, and candidate randomization. We impose this
continuation probability directly because the density assumption below is
insufficient by itself. It
ensures that the unpenalized source procedure continues whenever an admissible
candidate action remains. It is used in the proof to obtain the common path-depth
envelope. It places no ordering restriction on competing split gains.

For comparison define the linear orthogonal score
\begin{equation*}
\widehat\Gamma_i=4(W_i-1/2)\{Y_i-\widehat m(X_i)\}.
\end{equation*}
Its CART criterion is
\begin{equation*}
\widehat{\mathcal Q}_C^\Gamma(j,t)
=\widehat p_{C_L\mid C}\widehat p_{C_R\mid C}
 (\overline\Gamma_{C_L}-\overline\Gamma_{C_R})^2
\end{equation*}
over the same candidate library. Candidate counts and column samples are
independent across nodes and independent of the other
tree randomization. The estimation half supplies the observations used to
compute leaf means, and empty
leaves are pruned.

For a forest with \(B_n\) trees and an independent target \(x\), let
\(\alpha_i(x)\) be its nonnegative weights over nonempty honest leaves, with
\(\sum_i\alpha_i(x)=1\), and define
\[
\bar Z_\alpha(x)=\sum_i\alpha_i(x)Z_i,
\quad
\bar{\widetilde Y}_\alpha(x)=\sum_i\alpha_i(x)\widetilde Y_i,
\quad
\overline{Z\widetilde Y}_\alpha(x)=\sum_i\alpha_i(x)Z_i\widetilde Y_i.
\]
The source prediction is a local covariance ratio because the forest solves a
weighted treatment-effect moment inside the target's leaves. It is exactly
\begin{equation*}
\widehat T_{n,B_n}(x)
=\frac{\overline{Z\widetilde Y}_\alpha(x)
-\bar Z_\alpha(x)\bar{\widetilde Y}_\alpha(x)}
{1/4-\bar Z_\alpha(x)^2}.
\end{equation*}
Its forest-level linear comparator
\begin{equation*}
\check T_{n,B_n}(x)
=\sum_i\alpha_i(x)\widehat\Gamma_i
=4\overline{Z\widetilde Y}_\alpha(x)
\end{equation*}
is the forest average of the generic honest leaf scores
\(\widetilde T_{b,n}^R(x)\) defined in Section~\ref{sec:framework}. It satisfies
the exact decomposition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-prediction-remainder}
\widehat T_{n,B_n}(x)-\check T_{n,B_n}(x)
=\frac{4\bar Z_\alpha(x)
\{4\overline{Z\widetilde Y}_\alpha(x)\bar Z_\alpha(x)
-\bar{\widetilde Y}_\alpha(x)\}}
{1-4\bar Z_\alpha(x)^2}.
\end{equation}
The package reports \(\widehat T_{n,B_n}\) as its CATE estimate. The theorem
below shows that its probability limit can differ from the true CATE when the
representation changes candidate access.
Let \(\widehat T_{h,n}\) and \(\check T_{h,n}\), \(h\in\{1,2\}\),
denote the source forest and its linear comparator under \(R_{h,n}\), each
with \(B_n\) trees. The production-rate condition is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:production-rates}
\begin{gathered}
s_n\to\infty,\qquad s_n/n\le\bar s<1,\qquad
k_n\to\infty,\qquad k_n/s_n\to0,\qquad
k_n/\log(B_ns_n)\to\infty,\\
B_n\to\infty,\qquad
H_n:=\log(s_n/k_n)\to\infty.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The quantity \(H_n\) is the effective path depth. A sequence of roughly
balanced splits reduces a root sample of order \(s_n\) to the minimum leaf
scale \(k_n\) in order \(\log(s_n/k_n)\) steps.

\begin{assumption}\label{ass:source-regularity}
Uniformly over \(a\in\mathcal C_a\), the outcome is bounded,
\(U\mid X,W\) has a continuous density on a bounded support, the cross-fitted nuisance estimates
satisfy \eqref{eq:nuisance-rate}, and the source nondegeneracy condition
\eqref{eq:source-split-nondegeneracy} holds. Tree groups used for variance
estimation have uniformly bounded size, and the sequences
\((s_n,k_n,B_n)\) satisfy \eqref{eq:production-rates}.
\end{assumption}

Boundedness and the nuisance rate control the honest score. Split
nondegeneracy and the production rates support the leaf and forest
approximations. The appendix verifies
Assumptions~\ref{ass:semantic-score} and \ref{ass:honest-resolution} for the
source forest under these conditions.

\subsection{Main result: multiplicity and forest limits}\label{sec:resolution-intensity}

Define the unconstrained treatment rules
\begin{equation*}
\widehat\pi_{h,n}(x)=\one\{\widehat T_{h,n}(x)>0\},
\qquad
\pi_a^*(x)=\one\{\tau_a(x)>0\}=\one\{s_2(x)=1\}.
\end{equation*}

Let \(r_g\) count the representation columns in split-action class \(g\), and set
\[
\bm r=(r_1,\ldots,r_G)^\top,\qquad M=\sum_{g=1}^Gr_g.
\]
At node \(C\), class \(g\) has finite split-action set
\(\mathcal A_g(C)\), and let
\(\mathcal G(C)=\{g:\mathcal A_g(C)\ne\varnothing\}\) be the active classes.
For \(g\in\mathcal G(C)\) and \(u\in\mathcal A_g(C)\), write
\(\widehat{\mathcal Q}_C(u)\) for the realized source criterion indexed by split
action \(u\), and select the class optimum
\[
\widehat u_g(C)=\arg\max_{u\in\mathcal A_g(C)}
\widehat{\mathcal Q}_C(u)
\]
using a fixed deterministic tie rule. Write
\(\widehat v_g(C)=\widehat{\mathcal Q}_C\{\widehat u_g(C)\}\), let
\(G_C=|\mathcal G(C)|\), and let \(g_j(C)\) denote the active class with the
\(j\)th largest realized score. A fixed
representation-independent class order breaks ties, so
\[
\widehat v_{g_1(C)}(C)\ge\cdots\ge
\widehat v_{g_{G_C}(C)}(C).
\]
For a general candidate-count rule, let \(\nu_M(q)=\Prb(Q_M=q)\) on
\(\{1,\ldots,M\}\). Conditional on \(Q_M=q\), sample \(q\) columns uniformly
without replacement. For the class ranking defined above,
define cumulative multiplicity by
\[
M_{j,C}^{\rm cum}
=\sum_{\ell=1}^jr_{g_\ell(C)},\qquad
M_{0,C}^{\rm cum}=0.
\]
Conditional on the realized node state and this score order, the probability
that active class \(g_j(C)\), \(j=1,\ldots,G_C\), is chosen for the split is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:winner-fixed}
w_{j,C}(\bm r;q)
=
\frac{
\binom{M-M_{j-1,C}^{\rm cum}}q-
\binom{M-M_{j,C}^{\rm cum}}q
}{\binom Mq},
\end{equation}
where \(\binom aq=0\) for \(a<q\). Under candidate-count law \(\nu_M\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:winner-random}
w_{j,C}(\bm r;\nu_M)
=\sum_{q=1}^M\nu_M(q)w_{j,C}(\bm r;q),
\end{equation}
and class \(g\) is available with probability
\begin{equation}\label{eq:availability-random}
\omega_g(\bm r;\nu_M)
=\sum_{q=1}^M\nu_M(q)
\left\{1-\frac{\binom{M-r_g}q}{\binom Mq}\right\}.
\end{equation}
The winner probabilities need not sum to one when a candidate draw contains
only inactive classes. The remaining mass is the probability that the node has
no admissible sampled action.

Set
\begin{equation*}
\omega_n=\frac{\E Q_{M_n}}{M_n},
\qquad \mathcal I_n=H_n\omega_n.
\end{equation*}
Call \(\omega_n\) the singleton's per-node inclusion probability and
\(\mathcal I_n\) its cumulative-inclusion index.
For \(h\in\{1,2\}\), write \(\bar h=3-h\). In this design, success at a reached
node means that the chosen split uses singleton \(X_{\bar h}\). It is a property
of the node transition and does not depend on which evaluation point later
traverses the node. Conditional on the training state, construct a full
potential failure tree \(\mathcal T_{h,n}^0\) recursively. At each node, draw the
source transition from its regular conditional law given that the chosen split
does not use \(X_{\bar h}\), and generate both children with independent
node-address randomizers. Null conditional branches are absorbing. The
evaluation point \(X\sim P_X\) is drawn independently after this tree is built
and routed through it. This construction realizes the failure kernels in
Section~\ref{sec:framework} while retaining the original \(M_n\)-column
candidate law, stopping rule, and competing actions. No column is deleted.
Let \(J_{h,n}^0(x)\) be the number of internal nodes on the resulting path to
the terminal leaf. Let \(\rho_{h,n,t}^0(x)\) be the history-conditional hazard
that the singleton is selected and resolved at internal node \(t\), and set
\begin{equation*}
\Lambda_{h,n}^0(x)=\sum_{t=1}^{J_{h,n}^0(x)}\rho_{h,n,t}^0(x).
\end{equation*}
The leading index \(h\) identifies representation \(R_{h,n}\), while the
unresolved component is \(\bar h\). In the abstract notation,
\(S_{\bar h,n,t}^{R_{h,n}}(x)=1\) exactly when the chosen split uses
\(X_{\bar h}\), and
\[
J_{h,n}^0=J_{\bar h,n}^{R_{h,n},0},\qquad
\rho_{h,n,t}^0=\rho_{\bar h,n,t}^{R_{h,n},0},\qquad
\Lambda_{h,n}^0=\Lambda_{\bar h,n}^{R_{h,n}}.
\]
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} establishes threshold localization.

The counterfactual path evolves under repeated singleton failure and need not
coincide with an observed tree path. The index \(\mathcal I_n=H_n\omega_n\) combines its
two relevant scales. The path contains order-\(H_n\) split nodes, and the
singleton enters each candidate set with probability \(\omega_n\). Split
competition determines whether candidate inclusion resolves it. The proof establishes
the source-score transfer, honest reduction, depth bounds, and hazard
approximation. The intermediate formula additionally requires normalized path
depth to converge.

For a fixed \(a\in\mathcal C_a\) and a sequence with
\(\mathcal I_n\to\kappa\in(0,\infty)\), the intermediate regime additionally
requires the pointwise depth condition under the joint law \(\Prb_a^0\) of the
training sample, the full failure tree, and an independent \(X\sim P_X\) routed
through that tree,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intermediate-depth}
\frac{J_{h,n}^0(X)}{H_n}\xrightarrow[\Prb_a^0]{p}d_h(a)\in(0,\infty),
\qquad h=1,2,
\end{equation}
and we write \(\zeta_h(a)=\exp\{-\kappa d_h(a)\}\).

\begin{theorem}[Limits of causal-forest predictions]\label{thm:adaptive}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:source-regularity}, the following two endpoint results
hold uniformly over \(a\in\mathcal C_a\). If \(\mathcal I_n\to0\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:signed-selection-targets}
\max_{h\in\{1,2\}}
\|\widehat T_{h,n}-a_hs_h\|_{L^1(P_X)}
\longrightarrow_p0.
\end{equation}
If \(\mathcal I_n\to\infty\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:signed-recovery-target}
\max_{h\in\{1,2\}}
\|\widehat T_{h,n}-\tau_a\|_{L^1(P_X)}
\longrightarrow_p0.
\end{equation}
For any fixed \(a\in\mathcal C_a\), if
\(\mathcal I_n\to\kappa\in(0,\infty)\) and
\eqref{eq:intermediate-depth} holds under \(\Prb_a^0\), then
\begin{align}
\widehat T_{1,n}
&\longrightarrow_p
T_{1,\kappa}
=a_1s_1+\{1-\zeta_1(a)\}a_2s_2,
\label{eq:intermediate-target-one}\\
\widehat T_{2,n}
&\longrightarrow_p
T_{2,\kappa}
=\{1-\zeta_2(a)\}a_1s_1+a_2s_2
\label{eq:intermediate-target-two}
\end{align}
in \(L^1(P_X)\).
\end{theorem}

The product \(H_n\omega_n\) gives the expected order of singleton appearances
along the path. If it tends to zero, the singleton usually never enters the
search and its component drops out. If it diverges, the singleton is offered
often enough for recovery. At an interior limit, the depth condition
\eqref{eq:intermediate-depth} determines the remaining attenuation. The two
endpoint results hold without this additional condition. Across all three cases,
the representations differ only in how candidate access is allocated among
equivalent columns.

For a treatment rule \(\pi\), define
\[
V_a(\pi)=\E\{\tau_a(X)\pi(X)\},
\qquad
\operatorname{Reg}_a(\pi)=V_a(\pi_a^*)-V_a(\pi).
\]
The regret identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unconstrained-regret-identity}
\operatorname{Reg}_a(\pi)
=\E\!\left[|\tau_a(X)|
\one\{\pi(X)\ne\pi_a^*(X)\}\right]
\end{equation}
shows that a wrong sign is a treatment mistake weighted by its causal margin.

\begin{corollary}[Sign reversal and policy regret]\label{cor:sign-policy}
Under the conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:adaptive}, if
\(\mathcal I_n\to0\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:population-sign-reversal}
\left|
\Prb_X\{\widehat T_{1,n}(X)\widehat T_{2,n}(X)<0\}
-\frac12
\right|\longrightarrow_p0,
\end{equation}
and the reversal is separated from zero.
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strong-population-sign-reversal}
\Prb_X\left\{
\begin{array}{c}
\widehat T_{1,n}(X)\widehat T_{2,n}(X)<0,\\
\min(|\widehat T_{1,n}(X)|,|\widehat T_{2,n}(X)|)
\ge\underline a/2,\\
|\tau_a(X)|\ge\Delta_a
\end{array}
\right\}
\longrightarrow_p\frac12.
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{align}
\operatorname{Reg}_a(\widehat\pi_{2,n})&\longrightarrow_p0,
\label{eq:oracle-sign-policy}\\
\left|
\operatorname{Reg}_a(\widehat\pi_{1,n})
-\frac{a_2-a_1}{2}
\right|&\longrightarrow_p0.
\label{eq:sign-policy-regret}
\end{align}
\end{corollary}

Because \(a_2>a_1\), the representation that repeats \(X_2\) yields the
optimal unconstrained rule in this regime. Repeating \(X_1\) instead produces
limiting regret \((a_2-a_1)/2\). The corollary concerns unconstrained treatment
assignment. With a capacity constraint, selection depends on ranks around an
endogenous cutoff. Section~\ref{sec:evidence} examines the sensitivity of such
a ranking.

\subsection{Finite-dimensional extension}

For a fixed \(G\ge2\), let
\[
X=(X_1,\ldots,X_G)\sim\operatorname{Unif}([0,1]^G),
\qquad s_g=2\one\{X_g\ge1/2\}-1.
\]
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-g-coefficients}
\mathcal C_G=
\left\{
a\in[\underline a_G,\overline a_G]^G:
\min_{g\ne h}|a_g^2-a_h^2|\ge\Delta_G
\right\},
\qquad
\tau_a^{(G)}=\sum_{g=1}^Ga_gs_g,
\end{equation}
where \(0<\underline a_G<\overline a_G<\infty\) and
\(0<\Delta_G<(\overline a_G^2-\underline a_G^2)/(G-1)\), so
\(\mathcal C_G\) has nonempty interior.
For each \(g\), let \(R_{g,n}^{(G)}\) contain \(c_n\ge1\) deterministic strictly
increasing recodings of \(X_g\), including the identity, and one copy of every
\(X_h\), \(h\ne g\). Then
\(M_{G,n}=c_n+G-1\), and every representation generates \(\sigma(X)\). The
corresponding per-node inclusion probability and cumulative-inclusion index
are
\begin{equation*}
\omega_{G,n}=\frac{\E Q_{M_{G,n}}}{M_{G,n}},
\qquad \mathcal I_{G,n}=H_n\omega_{G,n}.
\end{equation*}
Run the same source learner and bounded randomized model with target
\(\tau_a^{(G)}\), under the corresponding fixed-\(G\) version of
Assumption~\ref{ass:source-regularity}. Write \(\widehat T_{g,n}^{(G)}\) and
\(\check T_{g,n}^{(G)}\) for the resulting prediction and linear comparator.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-dimensional component selection]\label{prop:finite-g}
Uniformly over \(a\in\mathcal C_G\),
\begin{align}
\mathcal I_{G,n}\to0
&\Longrightarrow
\max_{1\le g\le G}
\|\widehat T_{g,n}^{(G)}-a_gs_g\|_{L^1(P_X)}\longrightarrow_p0,
\label{eq:finite-g-selection}\\
\mathcal I_{G,n}\to\infty
&\Longrightarrow
\max_{1\le g\le G}
\|\widehat T_{g,n}^{(G)}-\tau_a^{(G)}\|_{L^1(P_X)}\longrightarrow_p0.
\label{eq:finite-g-recovery}
\end{align}
For every \(g\ne h\), the first regime also gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-g-pair-sign}
\left|
\Prb_X\{\widehat T_{g,n}^{(G)}(X)\widehat T_{h,n}^{(G)}(X)<0\}-\frac12
\right|\longrightarrow_p0.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-g} extends the mechanism from two effect modifiers
to any fixed number. When cumulative singleton inclusion vanishes, the
representation that repeats \(X_g\) selects the single component
\(a_gs_g\). The \(G\) information-equivalent representations can therefore
converge to \(G\) different component targets, and any pair disagrees in sign
on half the covariate space. When cumulative inclusion diverges, every
representation recovers the full target \(\tau_a^{(G)}\). The proposition keeps
\(G\) fixed and does not cover a growing number of effect components.

\begin{corollary}[Multiplicity boundary]\label{cor:critical-multiplicity}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:source-regularity}, for the source learner above, if
\(M_n\to\infty\) and \(\xi_{M_n}\equiv\xi\) for a fixed \(\xi>0\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-mtry-boundary}
\omega_n\sim\frac{\xi+e^{-\xi}}{M_n},
\end{equation}
so the critical multiplicity is \(M_n\asymp H_n\). Under package-default
\texttt{mtry} scaling
\begin{equation}\label{eq:default-mtry}
\xi_M=\min\{\lceil\sqrt M\rceil+20,M\},
\end{equation}
\(\omega_n\sim M_n^{-1/2}\), and the critical multiplicity is
\(M_n\asymp H_n^2\). Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:default-phase}
\frac{M_n}{H_n^2}\to\infty
\Longrightarrow \mathcal I_n\to0,
\qquad
\frac{M_n}{H_n^2}\to0
\Longrightarrow \mathcal I_n\to\infty.
\end{equation}
The same boundaries hold with \(M_{G,n}\) and \(\mathcal I_{G,n}\). Under
fixed \texttt{mtry}, \(M_{G,n}\asymp H_n\), whereas under
\eqref{eq:default-mtry}, \(M_{G,n}\asymp H_n^2\).
Every fixed finite representation is in the recovery regime.
\end{corollary}

Corollary~\ref{cor:critical-multiplicity} translates resolution intensity into
a comparison between path depth and raw dimension. With fixed \texttt{mtry},
a singleton enters a node's candidate set with probability of order
\(M_n^{-1}\), giving order \(H_n/M_n\) inclusions along a target path. Under
package-default scaling, this count is of order \(H_n/\sqrt{M_n}\). Above the
corresponding multiplicity scale, the singleton is unlikely to enter a candidate
set before the leaf is reached. Below that scale, repeated inclusion permits
recovery.

The phrase package default refers only to the \texttt{mtry} rule in
\eqref{eq:default-mtry}. The production sequence in
\eqref{eq:production-rates} continues to require \(k_n\to\infty\) and therefore
differs from repeated fits that hold the package's finite-sample node-size
setting fixed.

These boundaries compare sequences of representations as the sample size
grows. Equation~\eqref{eq:population-sign-reversal} concerns the
infinite-forest target. Finite-ensemble simulation noise is separate. In
particular, \eqref{eq:default-phase} cannot produce inconsistent limits from
two fixed finite representations.

\section{Finite-Array Class Sampling}\label{sec:quotient}

Ordinary \texttt{grf} samples raw columns. This section defines a new learner
that samples certified split classes. Group-first sampling also appears in
module-guided forests, where correlation networks define the groups
\citep{chen2013module}. Here, high correlation is insufficient because two
variables may encode different economic states. The correction begins with a
declared finite array
\[
\mathcal X_{\rm cert}=\{x_1,\ldots,x_N\}
\]
that contains every point used for splitting, nuisance fitting, prediction,
variance estimation, and policy ranking. The array is complete, with no
missing covariate entries. A column's certificate is its weak ordering of
\(\mathcal X_{\rm cert}\), identified with the reversed ordering. Equal
certificates generate the same unordered threshold bipartitions on every subset
of \(\mathcal X_{\rm cert}\), and the certificates can be computed before the
forest is grown.

Let \(K\) denote the number of certified classes. The class-sampled forest has
three steps. First, compute each column's integer
weak-rank vector, retain the lexicographically smaller of its forward and
reverse strings, and map every class to a canonical rank column. The
implementation uses exact certificate comparison in place of a probabilistic
hash. Second, draw \(\widetilde Q_K\) classes uniformly without replacement
and evaluate each distinct action once, where
\(\widetilde\nu_K(q)=\Prb(\widetilde Q_K=q)\) for \(q=1,\ldots,K\). Third,
index child
orientation, ties, and random numbers by the ordered certificate. These
operations make the certified split class the unit of randomization. Class
reduction occurs before the candidate draw, so candidates are sampled from
\(K\) classes. Deleting duplicate actions afterward would retain the ordinary
column-sampling probabilities.

For an active certified class \(g\in\mathcal G(C)\), let
\(h_g(C)\) be the
number of active certified classes ranked ahead of \(g\) by the score and
fixed tie rule. A sampled inactive class contributes no admissible action and
does not block an active class. The class-level winning probability is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quotient-hazard}
\widetilde p_g(C)
=\sum_{q=1}^K\widetilde\nu_K(q)
\frac{\binom{K-h_g(C)}q-\binom{K-h_g(C)-1}q}{\binom Kq}.
\end{equation}

For example, a raw variable, its logarithm, and a reversed unit conversion have
one certificate whenever they induce the same weak ordering and tie blocks on
the declared array. The class-sampled forest represents that certificate once
and samples from the reduced class matrix.

Fix \(\mathcal X_{\rm cert}\), the certification rule, and the class-level
candidate-count law \(\widetilde\nu_K\). Replace the raw array by its canonical
class matrix before fitting. The coupled learners receive the same outcomes,
treatments, weights, cluster and unit identifiers, policy capacity, and tie
keys. They also use the same class-indexed folds, tree randomizers, variance
groups, and policy-ranking randomizers. Every downstream operation is assumed
to be a measurable function of these common inputs.

\begin{proposition}[Class-sampled forest]\label{prop:quotient}
Under the common class-level coupling just described, any operation preserving the certified class
set and its canonical routing leaves the restriction of every tree partition to
\(\mathcal X_{\rm cert}\), every forest weight,
prediction, reported standard error, and selected set on
\(\mathcal X_{\rm cert}\) unchanged. This includes permutation, insertion or deletion
within a represented class, complementation, and recoding that preserves or
reverses the observed weak order and ties. Winning
probabilities are given by \eqref{eq:quotient-hazard} and do not depend on raw
within-class multiplicities.
\end{proposition}

Equality of weak sample orderings certifies threshold partitions on a fixed
finite array. If \(\mathcal X_{\rm cert}\) includes the evaluation points, the
predictions and policy-selected sets are exactly invariant there.
The implementation maps each class to a canonical weak-rank coordinate and
orders classes by certificate before calling the forest engine. Only exact
weak-order classes are combined. Correlated variables with different orderings
remain separate, and the certification supplies no guarantee at undeclared
points.
Under a common seed, two certified representations pass identical ordered
class matrices and the same candidate-count parameter to the engine. The zero
differences reported below verify this algorithmic equality. Predictive accuracy
and the validity of reported variance estimates remain separate questions.
In the empirical checks, \(\widetilde Q_K\) uses the capped-Poisson rule in
\eqref{eq:grf-q}, with the same numerical \texttt{mtry} mean as the corresponding
baseline fit and with the draw capped at \(K\).

\section{Finite-Sample Evidence}\label{sec:evidence}

\subsection{Monte Carlo}

The Monte Carlo measures whether equivalent representations imply opposite
treatment rules for the same individual and compares those disagreements with
ordinary seed variation. The theorem uses strictly increasing recodings,
bounded outcomes, and asymptotic learner sequences. The experiment also uses
order-reversing and rank recodings with Gaussian outcomes in finite samples. Draw
\(X\sim\operatorname{Unif}([0,1]^p)\) for \(p\ge6\),
\(W\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\),
and let \(s_j=2\one\{X_j>1/2\}-1\). The treatment effect and outcome are
\begin{align*}
\tau(X)&=0.20+0.65s_1-0.55s_2+0.10(X_3-0.5),
\\
Y&=1+X_4+0.5X_5^2-0.5X_6+W\tau(X)+\varepsilon,
\qquad \varepsilon\sim N(0,1).
\end{align*}
The remaining \(p-6\) coordinates are independent noise covariates.
For \(j\in\{1,2\}\), representation \(R^{(j)}_\ell\) contains \(X\) and \(\ell\)
added order-preserving or order-reversing encodings of \(X_j\). The library
contains changes of units, complements, logs, ranks, powers, and smooth
monotone maps. The replication README records the ordered library. On the
pooled training-and-evaluation array,
\(R^{(1)}_\ell\) and \(R^{(2)}_\ell\) generate the same information and the same
distinct threshold partitions. They differ only in which effect modifier has
more column labels in candidate sampling. None of these added encodings is
numerically identical to its source column.
Pooling is used only to construct the covariate recodings consistently on this
finite array. Because every map is strictly monotone or order reversing on its
source column, it preserves all distinct finite-array thresholds. The
construction concerns only this observed array. Future observations require a
separately specified preprocessing rule.

For evaluation observation \(i\), let
\(\widehat\tau_{j,\ell,i}=\widehat\tau_{j,\ell}(X_i)\) be the prediction under
\(R_\ell^{(j)}\), and write
\(\E_n h_i=N_{\rm eval}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\rm eval}}h_i\). The implied
unconstrained rule is
\(\widehat\pi_{j,\ell}(X_i)=\one\{\widehat\tau_{j,\ell,i}>0\}\). We report
three paired disagreement rates. Each compares instability between the two
representations. The experiment does not estimate bias, mean squared error, or
policy value.
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{SR}_0
&=\E_n\one\{\widehat\tau_{1,\ell,i}\widehat\tau_{2,\ell,i}<0\},\\
\mathrm{SR}_{0.10}
&=\E_n\one\{\widehat\tau_{1,\ell,i}\widehat\tau_{2,\ell,i}<0,
\min(|\widehat\tau_{1,\ell,i}|,|\widehat\tau_{2,\ell,i}|)\ge0.10\},\\
\mathrm{SR}_{\tau,0.25}
&=\frac{\E_n\one\{\widehat\tau_{1,\ell,i}\widehat\tau_{2,\ell,i}<0,
|\tau(X_i)|\ge0.25\}}
{\E_n\one\{|\tau(X_i)|\ge0.25\}}.
\end{align*}
Every cell averages twenty independently generated training samples of size
3,000, each paired with an independent evaluation sample of size 30,000.
Forests use \texttt{grf} 2.4.0, 2,000 trees, oracle nuisance functions, and
common data and forest seeds across representations. Each replication is
therefore paired across representations. Their different column counts prevent
a common seed from aligning every internal random-number stream.

\subsection{Cash-transfer ranking sensitivity}

The Kenya exercise asks how a hypothetical top-half ranking changes when
baseline covariates are recoded without changing their information. I fit the
forests in sample and rank households using out-of-bag predictions. Because the
experimental design includes saturation and interference, I interpret the
switching rates only as descriptive measures of ranking sensitivity. The data are the
public household files from the randomized cash-transfer experiment in Kenya
analyzed by Egger et al.\ \citeyearpar{egger2022}. The outcome is endline
per-capita consumption, treatment is the randomized transfer indicator, and
the sample contains 4,765 eligible households with baseline data in 653
villages. Sixteen baseline predictors describe
household composition, labor-market status, land and livestock ownership,
durable ownership, meals, and protein meals.

The outcome is the released winsorized PPP measure of per-capita consumption.
Continuous baseline variables are median-imputed and indicator variables are
zero-imputed. A weighted regression forest supplies the common outcome
nuisance estimate, and the weighted treatment mean supplies the propensity
nuisance. The replication README lists the exact predictors and
recodings.

Every fit uses \texttt{grf} version 2.4.0, 2,000 trees, village clusters,
survey weights, a common outcome nuisance estimate, the same seed, and
\(\texttt{mtry}=16\). In this implementation, \texttt{mtry} is the mean
\(\xi_M\) of the uncapped Poisson draw \(N_M\) in \eqref{eq:grf-q}. The source
then clamps that draw to obtain the candidate count \(Q_M\). Holding this mean
fixed isolates representation multiplicity and differs from recomputing the
package default after each augmentation. I rank households by their out-of-bag
causal-forest treatment-effect scores and select the top half, with household
identifiers breaking numerical ties. Let \(S_0\) be the selected set under the
canonical representation and \(S_R\) the selected set under recoding \(R\). The
reported switching rate is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kenya-switch}
\operatorname{Switch}(R)
=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N
\left|\mathbf 1\{i\in S_R\}-\mathbf 1\{i\in S_0\}\right|
=\frac{|S_R\mathbin{\triangle}S_0|}{N}.
\end{equation}
It is the fraction of households whose selected status changes and counts both
exits and entries. Each set contains exactly \(\lfloor N/2\rfloor\) households.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Feature subsampling makes raw column labels part of a forest's specification.
Two representations can contain the same covariate information and still
assign different probabilities to the same split actions. In the signed
\texttt{grf} design, cumulative candidate inclusion determines each effect
component's weight in the limiting target.

The inconsistent limits require multiplicity to grow with the sample. Every
fixed finite representation covered by the theorem recovers under its
conditions. Finite samples can nevertheless display large, well-separated
sign changes. In the Kenya diagnostic, author-constructed preprocessing-style
recodings change selected status for roughly 6 to 8 percent of households,
compared with a maximum of 0.55 percent among three alternative seeds.

Practitioners should treat equivalent encodings as part of the forest design
and report sensitivity to them. The class-sampled construction gives exact
invariance on any array where the split classes have been certified. Applying
the same guarantee to future points requires a defensible global routing rule,
and learning economically valid classes remains open.
